Paper 02 · The bridge to language

Fine-Tuning Language Models from Human Preferences

Before instruction following became the dominant story, preference learning was already being adapted to language-model outputs.

Stiennon et al.2019summarization

The contribution

The paper shows how a language model can be fine-tuned using human comparisons rather than a hand-designed metric. Humans compare model outputs; a reward model learns the comparison pattern; reinforcement learning then optimizes the policy.

\[\hat R_\theta(y\mid x)=\sum_t r_\theta(x,y_{\leq t})\]

Why it matters

It establishes the basic language-model version of preference-based reward learning: text outputs become the trajectories, and human judgments become the training signal.

Method in four moves

01 · Generate

A pretrained language model samples candidate outputs for the same prompt.

02 · Compare

Annotators rank outputs by the target quality, rather than assigning an absolute score.

03 · Predict

A reward model is trained to predict which output a human would prefer.

04 · Optimize

The policy is fine-tuned against the learned reward while staying close to the supervised model.

Problem

Automatic metrics such as ROUGE are imperfect proxies for human quality.

Signal

Pairwise preferences give a richer and more learnable target than a brittle scalar metric.

Legacy

This pipeline becomes the template later used for instruction-following language models.
