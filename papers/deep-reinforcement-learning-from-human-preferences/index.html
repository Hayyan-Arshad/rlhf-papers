01 · Sample behavior

The agent interacts with an environment and produces trajectory segments: short sequences of frames, actions, and outcomes. In the Atari experiments, the researchers work from pixels and hide the built-in score display so the feedback must describe the behavior, not the score.

What is new here?

There is no hand-written reward function telling the agent what matters.

Why pairs?

People are often better at saying “A is better than B” than assigning a numeric score.

What learns?

A neural reward predictor estimates which segment a person would prefer.

Why it matters

The paper reports useful behavior with feedback on less than 1% of agent interactions.

This is a teaching game, not a reproduction of the paper’s A3C training run. The paper tested Atari games including BeamRider, Breakout, Enduro, Pong, Q*bert, Seaquest, and Space Invaders, plus simulated robot behaviors.
