Paper 06 · The reward-model shortcut

Direct Preference Optimization

A reparameterization of the RLHF objective turns preference learning into a direct classification-style update for the language model.

Rafailov et al.NeurIPS 2023preference optimization

The central idea

Under a KL-constrained reward-maximization objective, the optimal policy and reward can be expressed in terms of one another. DPO substitutes that relationship into the preference likelihood, eliminating the separately trained reward model and online PPO loop.

\[\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DPO}}(\pi_\theta;\pi_{\mathrm{ref}})=-\mathbb{E}\left[\log\sigma\left(\beta\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y_w\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y_w\mid x)}-\beta\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y_l\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(y_l\mid x)}\right)\right]\]

What disappears?

No explicit reward-model training, no policy rollouts during preference optimization, and no PPO hyperparameter loop. The reference model remains important.

RLHF versus DPO

RLHF

Preference pairs → reward model → sampled rollouts → PPO policy update.

DPO

Preference pairs → direct policy classification loss relative to a reference model.

Shared foundation

Both methods learn from the same kind of chosen/rejected preference data.

Different engineering

DPO trades online RL complexity for a sensitive offline objective and reference-policy dependence.

Mathematical move

The reward-policy correspondence is used to eliminate the explicit reward variable.

Practical appeal

Training looks closer to ordinary supervised fine-tuning over preference pairs.

Important caveat

Removing PPO does not remove the need for careful data, evaluation, and distribution control.
