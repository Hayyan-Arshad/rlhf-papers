Paper 03 · RLHF in a complete language task

Learning to Summarize from Human Feedback

A clean demonstration that human preferences can replace an unreliable reference metric when the target is “a good summary.”

Stiennon et al.NeurIPS 2020TL;DR summaries

The pipeline

Start with supervised learning, collect comparisons between summaries, fit a reward model, and use PPO to optimize the language model. The paper evaluates both reward-model accuracy and summary quality.

\[\max_\pi\;\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\pi(\cdot\mid x)}[r_\theta(x,y)]-\beta\,\mathrm{KL}(\pi\Vert\pi_{\mathrm{SFT}})\]

The key lesson

What the training loss rewards and what people actually value can diverge. Human feedback is a way to move the objective closer to the intended quality.

What changed from earlier work?

Data

The work uses Reddit TL;DR, where summaries are short enough for large-scale comparison.

Model

The reward model learns from ranked summaries and becomes a learned proxy for human quality.

Optimization

PPO updates the policy while a KL penalty prevents it from drifting too far from the supervised model.

Evaluation

Human evaluations remain central: automatic metrics alone do not settle whether a summary is good.

Failure mode

A reward model can be exploited by a policy that finds high-scoring but low-quality summaries.

Transfer

The method transfers beyond the original Reddit domain to CNN/Daily Mail.

Practical pattern

SFT → preference data → reward model → PPO becomes the modern RLHF recipe.
